# Wave 2 — Creative Direction

## Selected territory: Living Proof

The portfolio behaves like a carefully maintained working proof: claims are followed by evidence, content survives re-composition, and change is visible without becoming spectacle.

“Proof” connects four truthful ideas:

- a design proof that is inspected and refined;
- engineering evidence and visual-regression checks;
- portfolio proof through demonstrated work;
- a document that can change without losing coherence.

The metaphor is behavioral, not decorative. Do not add crop marks, registration crosses, fake redlines, blueprint grids, terminal UI, or stationery effects.

## Visual principles

1. Typography establishes hierarchy before containers do.
2. Claims and their proof remain visibly connected.
3. The same content may recompose, but it must remain legible and continuous.
4. Use actual facts and product actions instead of fabricated screenshots or metrics.
5. One accent color identifies action, inspection, focus, or revision.
6. Motion explains alignment, dependency, or state change.
7. Every still frame works without animation.
8. Mobile is an intentionally edited reading order, not a collapsed desktop.

## Art direction

- A warm paper field supports long-form reading without gallery-white sterility.
- Carbon ink carries the content.
- Correction blue connects editorial revision to the native language of web interaction.
- Composition alternates between open editorial passages and one concentrated inverse proof chapter.
- Stable alignment anchors create continuity, but the grid is never drawn as decoration.
- Each project is organized as condition, intervention, and consequence or as a real functional sequence.

## Remove from the current design

- detached hero evidence rail;
- green status dot and generic status symbolism;
- tilted browser-window illustrations;
- invented product metrics;
- pills used as visual texture;
- serif-versus-monospace discipline symbolism;
- repeated kicker / heading / note section templates;
- decorative boxes and shadows.

## Acceptance criteria

- The premise is understandable within five seconds.
- Every accent-blue element is actionable, focused, inspected, or changing.
- Every major claim has nearby evidence.
- The experience feels calm before it feels clever.
- Replacing the content with lorem ipsum would break the motion concept; otherwise the motion is too generic.
- The brand concept never becomes more memorable than the work.
